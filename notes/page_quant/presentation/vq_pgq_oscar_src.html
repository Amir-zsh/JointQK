Three Ways to Compress a KV Cache

One question — a page of keys must shrink 8×; what do you store? — and three answers: OSCAR adapts a uniform grid to each token, group VQ (Samuel's study) replaces coordinates with learned codebook indices, and pgq (this repo) spends a bit budget non-uniformly across transformed rows. Each is explained step by step with its math, then compared on measured quality and speed — including a row-paired cross-repo comparison on shared evaluation rows.

1 · The shared problem, and what all three agree on

Per (layer, KV-head), a key cache \(K \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}\) (\(d = 128\)) must be stored at ≈ 2 bits per coordinate — 8× below bf16 — such that future queries' attention, \( \operatorname{softmax}(q K^{\top}/\sqrt d)V \), survives. Every method here reads its compressed form directly at decode (no fp16 re-materialization), and all were evaluated under one protocol: V = TurboQuant 2-bit, layer 0 fp16, 400-prompt calibration where calibration is used, LongBench F1 on Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B.

Two of the three (VQ and pgq) share the same first move — the calibrated Q-weighted PCA basis: since the damage a key error \(e\) does to logits is exactly \( \mathbb{E}_q[(q\cdot e)^2] = e\,\Sigma_Q\,e^{\top} \), keys are encoded as \( r = (k-\mu)F \) in a basis where plain squared error equals that query-weighted damage and energy concentrates in the leading coordinates. OSCAR uses a weaker, query-covariance + Hadamard rotation — swapping only the rotation under an identical quantizer costs it 5.9× in proxy error (measured in this repo). The three approaches then diverge on what redundancy they exploit:

2 · OSCAR, step by step

Intuition. OSCAR splits the offline/runtime line differently from the other two: its rotation is learned offline from calibration covariance spectra (that is the paper's title — Offline Spectral Covariance-Aware Rotation, Zhou et al., arXiv:2605.17757), but its quantizer learns nothing offline — no calibrated grids, scales, or codebooks. Instead every token carries its own tiny normalizer computed at runtime, so any token, however unusual, lands on a fixed 4-level grid reasonably scaled. Robustness through per-token adaptivity; the cost is that the normalizer is metadata on every token, fetched inside the attention loop — exactly the 32 bits/token that VQ and pgq replace with offline-calibrated codebooks/grids (0 and 3 bits of per-token metadata respectively).

1

Rotate

\( k' = k\,R \) with \( R = U_Q H \): the query-covariance eigenbasis composed with a Hadamard, per layer. Data-light (one eigendecomposition), query-aware only through second moments of \(q\), not the \(k\)-vs-\(q\) interaction.

2

Per-token standardize

Each token computes its own statistics and clips outliers: \( \tilde k_j = (k'_j - m_t)/s_t \) with \( m_t, s_t \) the token's own mean/std. This is the load-bearing trick — and the metadata source.

3

Lloyd-Max INT2

Every coordinate snaps to the 4-level optimal-Gaussian grid: \( c_j = \operatorname{arg\,min}_{i \in \{0..3\}} |\tilde k_j - \ell_i| \). Uniform 2 bits for every coordinate of every token — no allocation.

4

Store

Codes packed 4-per-byte (quarter-interleaved "crumbs", 32 B/token) plus the per-token \((s_t, z_t)\) — 32 bits of metadata per token-group. Decode: \( \hat k_j = s_t\,\ell_{c_j} + z_t \), then rotate back (folded).

5

Protect

First 64 (sink) and last 256 (recent) tokens stay bf16. Replication in this repo confirmed these windows are a requirement: without them sinks corrupt and quality collapses. All-in rate: ≈ 2.28 b/coord at 128k context, ≈ 3.47 at short context (the windows amortize).

3 · Samuel's group VQ, step by step

Intuition. A scalar quantizer treats each coordinate alone; but coordinates are correlated and their joint distribution has shape. Quantizing small vectors (groups of 4 coordinates) against a learned codebook captures three classical gains at once — correlation ("memory"), distribution shape, and sphere-packing — and decode becomes the cheapest primitive there is: a table lookup.

1

Basis + stratified grouping

Same QPCA basis \( r = (k-\mu)F \). Coordinates are then permuted by energy rank so that rank \(\rho\) goes to group \( \rho \bmod N_G \) (\(N_G = d/G = 32\) groups of \(G{=}4\)): every group gets one high-energy coordinate, one mid, two tail — no group hoards the spectrum. The permutation folds into \(F\)'s columns, so it is free.

2

Water-filled codebook sizes

Per-coordinate bits from reverse water-filling the QPCA energies \(\Lambda_j\): \[ b_j = \max\!\Big(0,\ \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2 \frac{\Lambda_j}{\theta}\Big), \qquad \theta \ \text{chosen so}\ \textstyle\sum_j b_j = 2d, \] then summed within each group and rounded: group \(g\) gets a codebook of \( K_g = 2^{b_g} \) entries, average held at 2.0 b/coord. Important groups get bigger codebooks.

3

Learn codebooks (offline)

Per (layer, head, group), Lloyd's k-means on calibration residual groups: \[ \min_{C_g}\ \sum_{t} \big\lVert r_g(t) - C_g[\,i_g(t)\,] \big\rVert^2, \qquad i_g(t) = \operatorname{arg\,min}_{c} \lVert r_g(t) - C_g[c] \rVert^2 , \] with empty-cluster reseeding (calibration has ~8k tokens/head vs up to 4096 centroids). An entropy-constrained variant (ECVQ: penalize assignments by \( \lambda(-\log_2 p_i) \)) was tested and landed within noise — honest negative, not shipped. Centroids stored in fp8.

4

Encode / decode

Encode = nearest-centroid search per group (calibration-static codebooks). Payload per token: \( \sum_g b_g = 256 \) bits — indices only, zero per-token metadata. Decode = index gather: \( \hat r_g = C_g[i_g] \).

5

Protect

fp16 band on the first 4 + last 32 prompt tokens (OSCAR's idea, sized 10× smaller): ≈ +0.12 b/coord at 4k context, shrinking as \(1/T\). This band is load-bearing: without it his Llama mean drops from 45.4 to 38.4.

Why this can beat scalar coding — the math of the three VQ gains. At a fixed rate, vector quantization of dimension-\(G\) blocks beats per-coordinate scalar quantization by (i) the memory gain — it codes the joint distribution, so residual correlation the basis missed still helps; (ii) the shape gain — codewords concentrate where the density actually lives (for a Gaussian source, worth up to 2.81 dB over a uniform scalar grid); and (iii) the space-filling gain — \(G\)-dimensional Voronoi cells pack space better than cubes (up to 1.53 dB as \(G \to \infty\), a fraction of that at \(G{=}4\)). The QPCA basis already removes second-order correlation, so (i) is small here — which is why his measured VQ sits near, not above, the entropy-coding frontier.

4 · pgq, step by step

Intuition. Don't spend bits equally: spend them where queries will look. Rotate so error = attention damage; mix the 64 tokens of a page with a DCT so redundant tokens become near-empty rows; then let a per-page rate–distortion optimizer hand each row one of 8 width profiles, from "6 bits on hot coordinates" down to "nothing". Full derivations live in the arc report; the five equations that define the method:

1

Metric & basis

\( \mathbb{E}_q[(q\cdot e)^2] = e\,\Sigma_Q e^{\top} \); the basis \( F = W^{\top}V \) (with \( \Sigma_Q = W^{\top}W \)) makes code-space squared error EQUAL this quantity — the allocator's numbers are attention damage by construction.

2

Page transform

\( Y = D\,R_{\text{page}} \), orthonormal DCT-II across the 64 tokens. Token-axis correlation is 0.70–0.79 at lag 1 and long-range; the DCT concentrates 76–83% of page energy into 16 of 64 rows, and stationarity makes the fixed DCT ≈ the optimal transform (within 2%).

3

Per-page RDO

Each row picks one of ≤8 monotone width profiles by a Lagrangian solved per page: \( \rho(t) = \operatorname{arg\,min}_{\rho} D_{t,\rho} + \lambda R_{\rho} \), with \(\lambda\) bisected until the page exactly fits its fixed byte budget (headers, ids, escapes all charged). Distortions are measured post-snap, so grid overload prices itself.

4

Grids & protections

Lloyd-Max levels scaled by per-(row, coord) calibrated \(\sigma\); positions 0–3 escape to an absolute 8-bit grid; the last 4 prompt pages ride the top rung. Both triggers are pure position — zero metadata.

5

Serve

\( \tilde q = q\,G^{\top} \) once per step; the fused kernel dots integer codes and applies \( z = D^{\top}(\hat Y \tilde q^{\top}) \) per page. Per-token metadata: a 3-bit rung id. Verified end-to-end: the quantized-resident decode is token-identical to the reference semantics (G2 = 1.000).

6 · Performance (measured)

How to read the numbers below (no prior context needed)

Every benchmark question gets an F1 score between 0 and 100 for each method (how well the generated answer matches the reference). To compare two methods we use row-paired differencing: both methods answered the same questions, so for each question \(i\) we compute the per-question difference \( \delta_i = F1^{A}_i - F1^{B}_i \), and report:

  • "mean Δ (A − B)" — the average of those per-question differences, in F1 points. Positive = method A better. "Pooled" means averaged over all shared questions across the three tasks (850 in total).
  • "95% CI" (confidence interval) — how far that average could plausibly move if we had drawn different questions. Computed by the percentile bootstrap, exactly this procedure:
    1. Start from the per-question differences of one task, \( \delta_1, \dots, \delta_n \) (e.g. \(n{=}500\) for lcc).
    2. For each of \( B = 10{,}000 \) rounds: draw \(n\) indices \( j_1, \dots, j_n \) uniformly with replacement from \( \{1..n\} \) (so some questions appear twice, some not at all — a simulated "alternative sample" of the benchmark), and record that round's mean \[ \Delta^{*}_{b} \;=\; \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta_{j_i} . \]
    3. For the pooled number, do step 2 for each task with its own rows and average the three task means within the same round: \( \Delta^{*}_{b} = \tfrac{1}{3}(\Delta^{*}_{b,\text{lcc}} + \Delta^{*}_{b,\text{2wiki}} + \Delta^{*}_{b,\text{musique}}) \) — tasks weighted equally, matching how the headline mean is formed.
    4. Sort the 10,000 values \( \Delta^{*}_{1..B} \); the reported interval is their 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles: \[ \text{95% CI} \;=\; \big[\, \Delta^{*}_{(250)},\ \Delta^{*}_{(9750)} \,\big]. \]
    No distributional assumption (no normality, no variance formula) — the spread of the resampled means IS the uncertainty estimate. The random seed is fixed, so the numbers are reproducible. If the interval contains 0 the data cannot rule out "no real difference" — we call that a tie (the point estimate is still the best guess). If it lies entirely above or below 0, the difference is significant (SIG).

Why pair instead of just comparing the two averages? Question difficulty varies enormously (some rows score 0 for every method, some 100), and that variation swamps method differences: Samuel's unpaired intervals are ±3.1 points wide — his top four methods are statistically indistinguishable that way. Differencing on the same question cancels the question's difficulty, leaving only the methods' disagreement; the intervals shrink to roughly ±1–1.6, narrow enough to rank methods. Pairing across the two repos is valid because we first verified the two evaluation harnesses agree: scoring OUR uncompressed run against HIS uncompressed run on the same questions gives −0.01 [−0.55, +0.55] — i.e. identical.

Diagnosis. Three different ceilings. The EC family is disqualified outright by serial decode — Samuel's numbers quantify the premise this whole repo was built on. 10 · Verdict & the obvious synthesis (original, pre-pgq10)

Quality: pgq ≥ VQ ≥ OSCAR. Row-paired, pgq@2.06 leads VQ@2.12 by +0.98 (CI ∋ 0) on every task, and pgq matches VQ with 14% fewer bits; both calibrated methods clearly beat OSCAR (~+2 F1 at ~60% of its bits). VQ's real achievement: EC-class quality with a constant-time decoder and zero per-token metadata.
Speed: OSCAR's kernels > pgq's kernel > VQ ≥ scalar ≫ EC. The format byte-ledger favors pgq; the kernel-maturity ledger favors OSCAR; VQ's gather decode is fundamentally cheap but not bandwidth-optimal.
Rate flexibility: only pgq has a per-page allocator, which is what turns its proxy wins into "same F1, fewer bits" — an axis neither uniform INT2 nor static codebooks can express.

The approaches are more complementary than competing:

  • VQ × DCT: his group-VQ quantizes token rows; our DCT produces coefficient rows with the same shape. K-means codebooks on DCT coefficient groups + our per-row RDO is a well-posed joint experiment — his codebook machinery, our transform and allocator, either repo's harness.
  • His throughput harness, our formats: a single-harness kernel shoot-out (his fused decode unit) would remove the two-harness caveat in §7.
  • Method hygiene both ways: our row-paired bootstraps sharpen his ±3 CIs into decidable contrasts (done above, on his own committed cells); his full-decode latency unit and H100 plan are the right next measurements for our kernel too.
