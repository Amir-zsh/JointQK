# KV-Cache Key Compression — Stage 1E Summary

We benchmarked 9 methods for compressing the K (key) side of the KV cache on Qwen3-8B over the LongBench-E 24-example bundle. Each method is a (basis × bit-allocation) pair: pick a linear basis to rotate keys into, then allocate a fixed bit budget across coordinates of that basis using uniform / hard-truncate / continuous water-fill.

**Winner: JointQK WaterFill.** A per-coordinate scalar quantizer in an orthogonal *joint-Q-K* basis — the eigenvectors of the symmetric matrix (Σ_Q Σ_K + Σ_K Σ_Q) / 2 — paired with reverse water-fill bit allocation. At 3 bits per coordinate (layer-0 excluded), it reaches **0.860** attention top-1 retention vs the prior best of **0.760** (Q-Eigen WaterFill, +10 pp). At 4 bits, decode-phase top-1 hits **0.944** — within 6 pp of full-precision attention. **Even at 2 bits per coordinate, JointQK WaterFill (0.767) approaches 4-bit TurboQuant (0.806) — within 4 pp at half the bit budget — and decisively beats 3-bit TurboQuant (0.682) by 8.6 pp.** The advantage is sustained at every bit budget (2/3/4), across three calibration sources (qasper, hotpotqa, passage_retrieval_en), across 24 leave-one-out folds, and from prefill-Q to decode-Q evaluation.

**Why classical CCA underperformed.** Canonical Correlation Analysis maximizes the *correlation* between Q and K projections — a scale-invariant objective that whitens away K-variance. KV-cache compression actually minimizes the Q-weighted reconstruction error of K, which is scale-dependent: per-coordinate logit error scales as (Q-energy × K-variance), not as canonical correlation. JointQK's basis orders coordinates by exactly that joint-energy product, directly matching the compression objective; CCA does not. Full derivation in §2.6 of the full report.

## Experiments at a glance

The study is structured as five experiments, each answering a distinct question.

**E1 — Canonical correlation spectrum diagnostic.** *Goal:* before any compression, ask whether a low-rank attention-relevant subspace actually exists in Qwen3-8B's Q/K second moments. *Take-away:* yes, but moderately so. The top 64 of 128 canonical-correlation directions capture ~94% of the joint Q-K energy in the median (layer, kv_head); the spectrum decays gradually with no sharp cliff. This rules out aggressive rank truncation and supports continuous bit allocation. Layer 0 is the visible outlier (steeper, attention-sink behaviour).

**E1_2 — Q/K distribution diagnostics across tasks.** *Goal:* understand *why* calibration generalizes between tasks. *Take-away:* the underlying second moments (Σ_Q, Σ_K, C_QK) barely shift between LongBench-E configs. Per-task cumulative-energy curves overlap nearly perfectly, and top-r eigenvector subspaces have ≥ 0.95 alignment between any two tasks across the rank range our methods care about. Calibration generalizes for distributional reasons, not just because the methods are shift-robust — a stronger guarantee.

**E3 — Real per-coordinate quantization (the headline).** *Goal:* head-to-head compare all 9 methods at 2/3/4 bits per coordinate using actual quantization, against actual prefill queries. *Take-away:* JointQK WaterFill wins on every metric (top-1, top-5, geometry distortion, logit MSE) at every bit budget, by a margin that *widens* at low bit budgets (+13.9 pp top-1 over Q-Eigen WaterFill at 2 bits, +10.0 pp at 3 bits, +8.2 pp at 4 bits). CCA-Orth WaterFill recovers +14.0 pp top-1 over the original CCA-NonOrth WaterFill, confirming that the dominant CCA pathology was the non-orthogonality of P_K, not the canonical-correlation basis itself. All four hard-truncate variants (rank=64) underperform their water-fill counterparts dramatically. The headline table and bit-budget sensitivity chart are in §3.3 of the full report.

**E4 — Generalization (cross-task + within-task LOO).** *Goal:* test whether the calibration-derived bases survive cross-task transfer (calibrate on one config, evaluate on the others — E4a) and whether dropping one example moves the metrics (within-task LOO, E4b). *Take-away:* JointQK WaterFill's cross-task top-1 spread is **0.2 pp** — even tighter than the calibration-independent TurboQuant baseline. Within-task LOO standard deviation is ≤ 0.009 top-1. The "calibrate offline once, deploy everywhere" pitch is empirically supported, and JointQK is the most stable method across both axes. See the cross-task heatmap and per-fold trace in §3.4.

**E5 — Decode-phase Q against compressed prefill cache.** *Goal:* the production scenario is "compress the prefill K cache, then generate against it." Verify that the prefill-only metrics from E3 transfer to the actual deployment regime. *Take-away:* decode-phase top-1 is *higher* than prefill-phase top-1 for every method at every bit budget — generated tokens have peakier attention distributions and tolerate more reconstruction noise. JointQK WaterFill at 3 bits reaches 0.904 decode top-1; at 4 bits, **0.944** (within 6 pp of full-precision attention). The decode-vs-prefill gap is *smallest* for JointQK because it's already so close to the ceiling. See §3.5.
