    An Interactive Companion

    Softmax-Aware QPCA for Attention-Preserving KV Compression

    A local Fisher surrogate for moving beyond average logit MSE

    ***

    Topic · KV-cache compression · attention geometry · source coding

    Goal · Improve the QPCA objective so it accounts for softmax attention

    Output · A closed-form surrogate, not an exact global optimum

    Plain QPCA minimizes the all-pairs squared logit reconstruction error $\sum_{i,j}(q_i^\top k_j - q_i^\top \hat{k}_j)^2$. That objective has a clean closed form, but it treats every query-key pair equally. Attention does not: softmax makes errors near the active competition set matter more than errors on already-irrelevant keys. This companion derives a softmax-local replacement based on the Fisher geometry of the categorical distribution. The result keeps the QPCA-style eigendecomposition while weighting the query and key statistics by attention sensitivity.
    Section 1

    Why logit MSE is not enough

    QPCA optimizes the right object only if every attention logit matters equally. A transformer layer uses the logits through softmax, where the local geometry depends on the current attention distribution.

    Plain QPCA objective

    For queries $q_i$ and keys $k_j$, QPCA minimizes $$ L_{\mathrm{logit}}(U, r) = \sum_{i,j} \left(q_i^\top k_j - q_i^\top U r_j\right)^2 . $$ The query distribution enters only through $M_q = \sum_i q_i q_i^\top$, which gives the closed-form basis from $M_q^{1/2} S_k M_q^{1/2}$.

    Problem

    Softmax is invariant to adding the same constant to every logit and is highly sensitive to changes near the top few competing keys. Average squared logit error penalizes harmless tail errors and important margin errors in the same quadratic way.

    Design target

    We want an objective that sees the complete attention score computation: scaling by $1/\sqrt{d}$, softmax normalization, and competition among cached keys. We also want to preserve enough structure to produce a calibrated basis, not a slow per-prompt optimization.

    Section 2

    The exact softmax objective

    The natural loss is attention-distribution divergence. It is faithful to the layer's computation, but it does not preserve QPCA's exact closed form.

    Attention distributions

    For a query $q_i$, define full and reconstructed logits $$ s_i(j) = \frac{q_i^\top k_j}{\sqrt{d}}, \qquad \hat{s}_i(j) = \frac{q_i^\top \hat{k}_j}{\sqrt{d}} , $$ and attention distributions $$ p_i = \operatorname{softmax}(s_i), \qquad \hat{p}_i = \operatorname{softmax}(\hat{s}_i). $$

    Faithful loss

    A direct K-side objective is $$ L_{\mathrm{attn}}(U,r) = \sum_i \operatorname{KL}\left( \operatorname{softmax}(s_i) \;\|\; \operatorname{softmax}(\hat{s}_i) \right). $$ This objective measures the actual attention probability distortion induced by compressing keys.

    No global QPCA-style closed form

    Exact $L_{\mathrm{attn}}$ couples all keys for each query through the softmax normalizer. Changing one reconstructed key changes the probability assigned to every other key. That coupling prevents the clean reduction to a single key second-moment matrix.

    Section 3

    Softmax KL has a Fisher quadratic

    Around the uncompressed logits, KL has a second-order approximation governed by the Fisher information matrix of the categorical distribution.

    Local KL expansion

    Let $p = \operatorname{softmax}(s)$ and perturb the logits by $\delta$. Then $$ \operatorname{KL}\left(\operatorname{softmax}(s) \;\|\; \operatorname{softmax}(s+\delta)\right) = \frac{1}{2}\delta^\top \left(\operatorname{Diag}(p) - p p^\top\right) \delta + O(\|\delta\|^3). $$

    Proof sketch

    The gradient of the cross-entropy in logits is $\hat{p} - p$, which is zero at $\hat{p}=p$. The Hessian of log-sum-exp at $s$ is $\operatorname{Diag}(p) - p p^\top$. Taylor expansion at $s$ gives the result.

    Interpretation

    The matrix $H(p)=\operatorname{Diag}(p)-p p^\top$ removes common-mode logit shifts and emphasizes keys with nontrivial attention probability. A tail key with $p_j \approx 0$ contributes little; a deterministic winner with $p_j \approx 1$ also contributes little because the distribution is locally saturated. The strongest signal occurs in the competition set.

    Section 4

    Applying the Fisher geometry to key errors

    Key reconstruction errors enter the softmax through logit errors. Substituting those logit errors into the local KL gives a softmax-aware K-side distortion.

    Logit perturbation from key error

    Write $e_j = \hat{k}_j-k_j$. For query $q_i$, $$ \delta_i(j) = \frac{q_i^\top e_j}{\sqrt{d}} . $$ The local attention loss is therefore $$ L_{\mathrm{Fisher}} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_i \delta_i^\top H(p_i)\delta_i . $$

    Expanded form

    Expanding the Fisher quadratic gives $$ L_{\mathrm{Fisher}} = \frac{1}{2d} \sum_i \left[ \sum_j p_{ij}(q_i^\top e_j)^2 - \left(\sum_j p_{ij}q_i^\top e_j\right)^2 \right]. $$ The first term is diagonal over keys. The second term is a covariance correction that couples errors across keys.

    The coupling term

    The covariance correction is exactly what makes the full Fisher objective non-separable across cached keys. A closed-form QPCA-like basis requires either modeling cross-key correlated quantization noise or dropping/approximating this term.

    Section 5

    A separable Fisher-QPCA surrogate

    To recover a practical closed-form basis, approximate the Fisher quadratic by keeping per-key sensitivity and discarding cross-key error covariance.

    Softmax sensitivity weights

    Define $$ \beta_{ij} = p_{ij}(1-p_{ij}), \qquad \alpha_j = \sum_i \beta_{ij}. $$ The scalar $\beta_{ij}$ is the diagonal Fisher sensitivity for key $j$ under query $i$.

    Separable approximation

    The diagonal-Fisher surrogate is $$ \tilde{L}_{\mathrm{Fisher}} = \frac{1}{2d} \sum_{i,j} \beta_{ij}(q_i^\top e_j)^2 . $$ This keeps the softmax competition weighting while making the loss additive over individual key errors.

    Why this is reasonable for quantization

    Scalar quantization errors are mostly local and per-token. If errors for different cached keys are treated as weakly correlated, the omitted cross-key covariance term is smaller than the diagonal sensitivity term. This is an approximation, but it is targeted at the part of the softmax geometry that plain logit MSE entirely misses.

    Section 6

    QPCA-style closed form under the surrogate

    The separable objective becomes QPCA again, but with attention-sensitive moments instead of uniform all-pairs moments.

    Fisher-weighted moments

    A direct per-key form uses $$ M_j = \frac{1}{d}\sum_i \beta_{ij} q_i q_i^\top . $$ For a single global basis per layer/head, use the pooled approximation $$ M_F = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{i,j}\beta_{ij} q_i q_i^\top, \qquad S_F = \sum_j \alpha_j k_j k_j^\top . $$

    Fisher-QPCA surrogate codec

    Let $$ A_F = M_F^{1/2} S_F M_F^{1/2} = V\Lambda V^\top , $$ with eigenvalues in descending order. The QPCA-style full-rank row-vector maps are $$ F_{\mathrm{soft}} = M_F^{1/2}V, \qquad G_{\mathrm{soft}} = V^\top M_F^{-1/2}. $$ Keys are encoded as $r = kF_{\mathrm{soft}}$ and reconstructed as $\hat{k}=rG_{\mathrm{soft}}$.

    Bit allocation

    As in QPCA, the weighted key error becomes approximately plain code-space error: $$ (k-\hat{k})^\top M_F(k-\hat{k}) \approx \|r-\hat{r}\|_2^2 . $$ The code-space per-coordinate variance is $\Lambda$, so the first allocation to test is water-fill on $\Lambda$ alone.

    Section 7

    Calibration procedure

    The procedure is close to the current QPCA implementation, with one extra pass that computes softmax probabilities on calibration raw Q/K tensors.

    Procedure
    1. For each layer and KV head, gather grouped queries $q_i$ and keys $k_j$.
    2. Compute logits $s_i(j)=q_i^\top k_j/\sqrt{d}$ in chunks.
    3. Compute $p_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}(s_i)_j$ and $\beta_{ij}=p_{ij}(1-p_{ij})$.
    4. Accumulate $M_F=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i,j}\beta_{ij}q_iq_i^\top$.
    5. Accumulate $S_F=\sum_j\alpha_jk_jk_j^\top$, where $\alpha_j=\sum_i\beta_{ij}$.
    6. Regularize $M_F$, eigendecompose $M_F^{1/2}S_FM_F^{1/2}$, and store forward, inverse, and eigenvalues.
    Numerical points

    The softmax pass should be chunked over queries to control memory. Very sharp attention can make $\beta$ small for most positions; use token-count and trace diagnostics so the effective sample size is visible. As with QPCA, compute square roots and inverse square roots in fp64, then cast stored maps to fp32.

    Decode queries

    If decode-time query captures are available, build a second basis or blend prefill and decode statistics. The softmax weights are only as relevant as the query distribution used to compute them.

    Section 8

    Expected tests and failure modes

    Fisher-QPCA is an attention-aware surrogate. It should be judged by attention metrics first, not by raw K MSE.

    Metric Reason
    Softmax KL Directly measures the local objective the basis was designed to reduce.
    Top-1 / top-5 retention Checks whether lower softmax distortion preserves attention competition.
    Logit error Verifies whether attention weighting trades away average logit MSE.
    Downstream F1 Final arbiter before replacing JointQK in production sweeps.
    Failure modes

    The diagonal Fisher surrogate can overweight ambiguous mid-probability keys and underweight deterministic high-confidence winners. It can also become noisy if computed from too few calibration prompts. If top-1 falls while KL improves, the next variant should add margin-aware weights around the original argmax.

    Practical conclusion

    The exact softmax objective is the right target but not the right closed-form computation. The Fisher-QPCA surrogate is the conservative next step: it keeps a single eigendecomposition per layer/head while using the attention distribution to decide which query-key errors matter.

    Closing

    References and closing notes

    This companion should be read as a proposal for the next calibrated basis family, not as a replacement for empirical validation.

    Relationship to QPCA

    Plain QPCA is recovered when every query-key pair receives equal weight. Fisher-QPCA replaces equal all-pairs weighting with local softmax sensitivity. It is therefore a minimal extension: same map convention, same source-coding tail, different calibration moments.

    Final summary

    Exact attention preservation is nonlinear and globally coupled. A useful closed-form approximation comes from the second-order KL geometry of softmax: compute attention-sensitive moments, eigendecompose $M_F^{1/2}S_FM_F^{1/2}$, and evaluate the result on softmax KL, top-k retention, and downstream F1.
